    Reading the notation

    A letter names a face: Up, Down, Left, Right, Front, Back. A bare letter is a quarter turn clockwise as you look straight at that face. An apostrophe reverses it, a 2 means a half turn.

    R  =  right face, clockwise
    R'  =  right face, anticlockwise
    R2  =  right face, half turn

    Engine & maths

    What is actually running underneath the cube.

    No libraries

    Everything here is in this one file: the 3D renderer, the maths, the solver. Nothing is fetched from a network, so it runs offline and straight off a disk. Faces are culled, depth sorted and flat shaded onto a 2D canvas — and because a cube is built from flat faces, flat shading is not an approximation, it is exact. Only the specular highlight varies across a face, and that is drawn as a two point gradient.

    One geometry, two views

    A cube state is 54 stickers. Every face turn is a permutation of those 54 numbers — but the permutation tables are not typed in by hand. On load, each sticker is given a 3D lattice position and an outward normal, then rotated by an integer rotation matrix, and the resulting index is read back. The animation rotates the same pieces by the same angle about the same axis, so the picture and the logic come from a single source and cannot disagree.

    clockwise turn of face f = rotation of −90° about f's outward normal
    newState[i] = oldState[perm[i]]

    After each animated turn the 26 pieces are snapped back to identity and re-coloured from the logical state. Because the pieces are geometrically identical, that reset is invisible — and it removes any chance of drift building up over a hundred turns.

    Is this cube even possible?

    Only about one in twelve colourings of a cube can be reached by turning a real one. Three invariants have to hold, and RUBICEzy checks all three before it searches:

    Σ corner twists ≡ 0 (mod 3)
    Σ edge flips ≡ 0 (mod 2)
    sign(corner permutation) = sign(edge permutation)
    • Break the first and one corner is twisted in place — impossible without prising a sticker off.
    • Break the second and one edge is flipped in place.
    • Break the third and exactly two pieces have been swapped.

    The whole group has order 43,252,003,274,489,856,000, which is 8!·3⁷·12!·2¹¹/2: the divisions by 3, 2 and 2 are exactly those three invariants.

    How the solution is found

    Eight phases, in the order a person would learn them.

    • White daisy and cross. Each white edge is placed by iterative-deepening depth-first search over all 18 turns, with a goal test that also demands the previously placed edges stay put. Branching is cut from 18 to about 13.5 by refusing to turn the same face twice in a row and fixing an order for opposite faces.
    • First layer corners and middle layer. Deterministic. The cube is examined, one of a handful of named algorithms is chosen, and the algorithm is re-lettered for whichever slot is being filled — F→R→B→L→F — so one written sequence covers all four positions.
    • The last layer. Breadth-first search where each edge of the graph is a whole algorithm rather than a single turn, with a visited set. The goal test is "every sticker is home except this specific handful". That makes the search self-checking: a mistyped algorithm can only make the search slower, never make the answer wrong, because a state that damages the finished layers simply fails the test.

    Finally the turn list is reduced (R R' vanishes, R R becomes R2) and replayed against the starting state to confirm it really finishes solved. On load, twelve random scrambles are solved and verified the same way — that is the count in the header.

    The result is around 100–130 turns. A two-phase optimal solver would use about 20, but those 20 turns cannot be explained, and explaining is the point here. God's number, for the record, is 20 in the half-turn metric.

    Motion

    Dragging builds a rotation quaternion in camera space and pre-multiplies it onto the cube, which is why the cube keeps spinning the way your finger went no matter how it is already oriented. Letting go leaves angular velocity behind, decayed by a constant factor each frame — a spun cube coasts and settles. Turns ease with a cubic that overshoots by about five percent and returns, which reads as the click of a real mechanism.

    q ← Δq · q   (camera-space pre-multiply)
    ω ← 0.93·ω   (per frame, when not dragging)
